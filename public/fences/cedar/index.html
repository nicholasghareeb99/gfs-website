Privacy run, open entrance

If you like a tall privacy run with a shorter picket entrance, mark the transition on the layout. Compare the top profile, post caps and view through the gate from both sides before settling on the combination.

A gate has two finished views

Choose the side that presents the pickets or boards, then review where the rails, brace, latch and hinges will appear. A front-facing inspiration photo may leave those useful details out; the gate photos above show another view.

Make a more informed choice

Cedar: the details that matter

Species belongs in the proposal

Western red cedar has natural resistance to decay and insects. That is a species property, not a promise that every cedar component or installation lasts the same way. Ask which lumber is proposed for boards, rails and posts.

Color is a choice you maintain

Unfinished western red cedar weathers toward silver-gray. A finish can retain or change its appearance, but exposure and surface preparation affect the result. Decide whether weathering is welcome before treating color change as a defect.

More opacity means less visible grain

A semi-transparent stain leaves more wood character visible. Solid-color stains and paint hide more of the wood's natural color. Compare the look of the actual finish, not just a small color name on a chart.

Fasteners affect the finished appearance

Cedar can develop dark staining where incompatible iron contacts the wood. Fastener compatibility is worth including in the specification, especially when a clean, natural finish is part of the design.

A decorative top changes more than the silhouette

Lattice or an open top adds detail while leaving sightlines through that part of the fence. Check the view from seated eye level and from the neighboring side before choosing how much of the panel is open.

Bring these questions to your estimate

  1. Which cedar species and component materials will the proposal identify?
  2. Do I want the fence to weather naturally or keep a particular finished color?
  3. Which face will show rails, and how will the gate match it?
  4. Should the top line follow the yard's grade or step between sections?

Good questions. Clearer answers.

Does a cedar fence need the same finish on both faces?

Discuss the whole board and both fence faces when specifying a finish. Western Red Cedar Lumber Association guidance favors coating all surfaces before installation where practical; an installed fence may have less-accessible faces. State what the finishing scope actually includes.

Can I keep the grain visible and still change the color?

A semi-transparent stain can change the tone while allowing grain to show. More opaque coatings conceal more of the original appearance. Check a sample and follow the selected product's preparation and application directions.

Does gray cedar automatically mean the fence needs replacement?

No. Surface weathering and structural condition are different questions. Ask for the posts, rails, fasteners and boards to be assessed separately before choosing between cleaning, refinishing, repair or replacement.

Western Red Cedar is standard. Northern White Cedar is the premium option.
Posts in clay soil
8 ft galvanized steel, 2½ in. diameter, driven 4 ft into the ground. That is below the roughly 3 ft frost line here, and clay is most of the Toledo area.
Post finish
A wood box is built around each steel post, so you see wood, not steel.

Pressure-treated 4×6 set in concrete. Galvanized steel set in concrete is available as an upgrade.

Pickets
