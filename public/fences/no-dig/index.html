From layout to final walkthrough

How a driven-post fence comes together.

  1. 01

    Plan the line and gates

    We review where the fence will run, how gates will be used and what access the installation needs. The shared site plan gives you a clear starting point.

  2. 02

    Check the site and post system

    Before driving begins, confirm the required approvals and utility locating. Review ground conditions, obstructions, post spacing and the support detail for the selected fence.

  3. 03

    Drive the steel supports

    A post driver advances the steel support into the ground. The required embedment and installation procedure come from the selected system and site requirements—not a one-depth-fits-all rule.

  4. 04

    Fit and align the post assembly

    For vinyl, fit the Ridge X adapters and vinyl sleeve to the steel pipe. For King Plus wood fencing, build the U-shaped wood box, fit it around the driven support and align it before adding the rails. Follow the fastening detail selected for the project.

  5. 05

    Build the fence and fit the gates

    Attach the selected panels or rails and fit the gate hardware. Gate posts and wide openings receive the support detail required for their loads.

  6. 06

    Walk through the finished project

    Review the fence line, finish and gate operation together. Keep the shared scope, plan and questions with your project in the customer portal.

A smaller excavation footprint

Your new fence. Less mess in your yard.

No pile from a dug post hole.

At a directly driven support, there is no full post hole to excavate and no hole-sized pile of soil to spread or haul away. That keeps more of the surrounding lawn in place.

Skip the concrete handling.

Where the approved system uses no poured footing, there is no concrete to mix, move or wait to cure at that post. Removal work, gates or other site details may still need excavation.

Less disruption around each post.

A narrow driven support avoids opening a larger footing-sized hole. We still plan access, protect the work area and clean up after installation—especially when an old fence is being removed.

The support is more than the finish you see

Steel at the core. More choices on the outside.

Ridge X adapters inside the vinyl.

GFS uses Ridge X adapters from Central Fence Supply between the steel pipe and vinyl post sleeve. The composite adapters have openings for rail clearance and a square outer fit that helps keep the sleeve aligned. The pipe, adapters, fasteners, rails and panels work together as a system.

Strength comes from the complete specified assembly: steel size and wall thickness, embedment, soil, connections, fence height and gate loads. Some conventional vinyl systems also use reinforcement, so we compare the actual systems rather than the outer appearance alone.

The King Plus wood-box method.

GFS uses the King Plus method for wood fencing: one picket and two vertical 2×4s form a U-shaped box around the driven steel support. We build and align the box before attaching the fence rails. The steel provides the ground-contact support; the wood components still need appropriate care.

The ground line is an important place to inspect a wood post for decay and loss of section. A protected steel post addresses that wood-decay mechanism; its coating, connections and condition still matter over time. Galvanizing provides zinc protection, while soil conditions and coating condition influence corrosion performance.

The right method starts with the site

Careful placement starts with knowing what is below.

We plan individual post positions around the confirmed site constraints before installation. A narrow support uses a smaller footprint than an excavated footing, but it cannot detect or safely pass through a buried utility.

No-dig still disturbs the ground. Before posts are driven, the crew needs the required 811 notification, utility responses and any necessary private-line locating. Near marked lines, follow the required clearance and exposure procedures; change the layout or method when needed.

  • Review loose fill, rock and hidden obstructions.
  • Match supports to the fence system, height and gates.
  • Check public utilities and private lines such as irrigation, pool equipment and power to a shed.
  • Confirm property information, easements and approvals.
  • Agree on any locations that need a different detail.

Think beyond the first set of panels

A future fence may start with posts you already have.

If the steel supports remain sound and suit the next fence, it may be possible to replace boards, rails or panels while retaining them. That can avoid some post removal, disposal and reinstallation work.

1. Inspect what remains.

Check corrosion, movement, damage, alignment and connections. A post that is leaning or deteriorated is not automatically a candidate for reuse.

2. Match the new design.

Confirm spacing, height, gate loads, fittings and the support requirements for the replacement fence. A change of style may need different posts.

3. Price the work you need.

Compare a reuse option with a full replacement in the written proposal. Savings depend on how much sound structure can actually stay.

A durable steel support can outlast replaceable wood components. Future reuse and lower replacement cost are possibilities to evaluate—not a lifetime guarantee or a fixed discount.

Questions about no-dig installation.

What does “no-dig” mean?

The fence support is driven into the ground instead of being placed in an excavated, concrete-filled post hole. It describes the post installation method. The fence material and finish are separate choices.

Can every fence use driven posts?

No. The selected post system, soil, obstructions, fence height and gate loads all matter. We review the proposed method for your project and explain where a different foundation detail is needed.

Does no-dig mean no site checks?

No. The layout, property-line information, easements, underground utilities and required approvals still need review before installation. Parcel mapping helps with planning but does not replace a boundary survey.

Can I choose privacy or an open fence?

The finished style depends on the compatible fence system. Tell us whether you want privacy, an open view or a particular gate, and we will review suitable materials and support details together.

Will there be any mess at all?

Directly driven supports avoid the excavated dirt piles and concrete handling associated with those post holes. Old-fence removal, access, obstructions and other project details can still disturb the yard. The proposal should describe removal and cleanup.

Does steel make every no-dig fence stronger?

Steel reinforcement is part of the support system, but performance depends on the complete assembly and installation. Ask which post, coating, fittings and gate details are specified. Compare any tested rating only for the exact system and conditions it covers.

Can the steel posts stay when the fence is replaced?

Possibly. Retaining sound, compatible supports can reduce replacement work. Their condition, spacing, alignment and suitability for the new fence need inspection before reuse or savings can be confirmed.

Is no-dig a way to skip utility locating?

No. A driven post can damage buried utilities. Required utility notification, responses, private-line locating and safe working procedures still apply before installation.

How this installation overview was prepared

GFS has confirmed Ridge X adapters for vinyl and the King Plus method for wood. Central Fence Supply describes the Ridge X adapter family and publishes fastening instructions and warranty conditions.
