Make a more informed choice

Pressure-treated wood: the details that matter

Read the treatment label, not just the color

AWPA categories identify intended exposure. UC3B describes exterior above-ground use; UC4A describes general ground contact. A fence post and an above-ground picket may need different specifications. Site conditions determine the appropriate selection.

Treatment does not stop drying movement

Wet pressure-treated pickets can shrink as they dry. A close fit on installation day may become a visible gap. Compare the intended board spacing or overlap with your privacy expectations rather than expecting the original fit never to change.

Finish when the wood is ready

Stain or sealer needs a suitably dry, prepared surface. There is no single waiting period that fits every treated board and exposure. Confirm the lumber condition and the coating manufacturer's instructions before scheduling a finish.

The pattern should match the viewing angle

Standard privacy uses boards on one face. Board-on-board uses overlap. Shadowbox alternates boards and allows angled views through the fence. Compare these at the places where you sit or walk, not only straight-on in a photograph.

Compare the support system separately

A quote for wood boards does not tell you the post material, rail layout, fasteners or gate support. Put those components beside the chosen board pattern so two estimates describe the same complete fence.

Bring these questions to your estimate

  1. Which treatment/use category is specified for the posts and other exposed components?
  2. How much angled visibility is acceptable from my patio?
  3. Is finishing included, excluded or quoted as a later step?
  4. What post, rail and gate construction accompanies each board-pattern option?

Good questions. Clearer answers.

Why can gaps appear in a new treated-wood fence?

Pressure-treated pickets installed while moist can narrow as they dry. The material condition, board layout and overlap influence the visible result. Ask how the proposed design accounts for movement before selecting the privacy pattern.

Is ground-contact treatment the same as a wood grade?

No. A treatment Use Category describes the exposure the preserved wood is intended for. It does not by itself identify lumber grade, appearance or dimensions. Ask for both the lumber and treatment details in the proposal.

Can staining be included without promising an exact date?

Yes. The scope can name the finish, surfaces and preparation while making application depend on suitable wood and weather conditions. That is more useful than assuming every newly installed treated board will be ready on the same day.
