Pet fencing: the details that matter

Height is only one escape route

Dogs can climb, dig, chew or learn to open gates. Describe the actual behavior rather than choosing a fence solely from height or breed. That helps focus the plan on the way the animal interacts with the boundary.

Nearby objects can become a launch point

A chair, storage box or doghouse beside the fence can make escape easier. Look at the usable yard layout as well as the fence itself, and keep climb-assisting objects away from the boundary.

The widest gap is the one to review

Check panel openings, the space under the fence, hinge gaps and the meeting point of a double gate. A tight panel specification does not eliminate a larger opening elsewhere in the enclosure.

A digger needs more than extra panel height

Ask about a suitable bottom detail and address why the dog is digging. Humane World for Animals recommends identifying escape incentives and seeking behavior help when needed. Any added ground barrier should be specified for the site and animal.

Design for how people use the gate

Identify the gate used by children, visitors and lawn services. Discuss a separate everyday entry from a large equipment opening if that fits the yard. Agree on a household routine for checking that the gate is actually secured.

Bring these questions to your estimate

  1. How has this dog escaped or tested barriers before?
  2. What are the dog's current size and likely adult size?
  3. Where are the largest ground, panel and gate openings in this layout?
  4. Who uses each gate, and how will it be checked after a visitor or service call?

Good questions. Clearer answers.

Should I plan for my puppy's current size or adult size?

Consider both. The current size matters for gaps it could pass through now, while adult size and behavior matter for the future. A height choice alone does not answer those two questions.

Would a taller fence solve a digging problem?

It does not address the path beneath the fence. Review the bottom detail and the reason for digging, then discuss an appropriate physical and behavioral plan. Supervise the dog while an escape issue is being resolved.

Can a privacy fence replace supervision?

No. A fence is one part of managing the yard. Check gate operation and new gaps, remove nearby launch points and pay attention to the dog's behavior. No fence design makes every pet unable to escape.
