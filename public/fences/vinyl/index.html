More than a privacy fence

Three ways to make vinyl yours.

Privacy fencing

Create a more private space.

Solid panels screen the yard from view. Compare top details, color combinations and the gate openings you need. Lattice-top designs add a decorative opening above the solid section.

Explore project photos ↓

Picket & open styles

Keep the yard connected to the view.

Spaced pickets define an edge while letting you see through. Compare open or closed tops, picket spacing and gate details. If the fence is for pets or a pool, review the complete layout and required hardware for that use.

Explore project photos ↓

Ranch rail

Mark the boundary without screening it.

Horizontal rails give a more open look along a lawn or larger property edge. Wide spaces between rails are part of the design; animal containment may require a compatible mesh or a different system.

The details behind your fence

Look inside a steel-supported vinyl post.

The visible sleeve is only one part of the assembly. See how the steel, adapters and rails fit together.

  1. Finished post

    The vinyl sleeve provides the finished appearance. Our no-dig vinyl system uses Ridge X adapters between the sleeve and steel support.

  2. Reveal the core

    The driven steel support continues into the ground. The compatible adapters help position the sleeve around it.

  3. Rail connection

    Rail openings and adapter placement must work together. The specified gate-post assembly may differ from a line post.

Make a more informed choice

Vinyl: the details that matter

Low upkeep still includes cleaning

Outdoor vinyl can collect dirt, surface chalk and mildew on accumulated grime. Manufacturer care guidance commonly starts with gentle washing and rinsing. Use the instructions for your actual product before trying stronger cleaners.

Rackable is a specification, not a material promise

A rackable panel changes angle to follow a slope. Manufacturers publish different limits for different systems. Confirm the chosen panel's allowance and the resulting gap at ground level rather than assuming all vinyl follows every grade.

A gate post may be built differently

Some vinyl systems call for reinforcement at gate posts even when ordinary line posts do not. Ask which support and hardware belong to the proposed gate so a matching appearance does not hide an incomplete scope.

Do not assume a later color change is simple

Painting restrictions are product-specific; some manufacturers advise against it and tie it to warranty terms. Choose the supplied color carefully and check the actual manufacturer's policy before planning to repaint.

Keep the product identity for future repairs

Record the panel style and component system with your project paperwork. If a section is later damaged, those details help identify a compatible part instead of trying to match white or tan by eye alone.

Bring these questions to your estimate

  1. Do I want solid privacy, an open picket style or an accent at the top?
  2. What is the selected panel's racking limit on my measured slope?
  3. What support is specified at the gate posts?
  4. Which cleaning and color-change instructions come with the product?

Good questions. Clearer answers.

Will a vinyl fence follow my sloping yard?

That depends on the panel system and the measured slope. Ask whether the proposed fence racks, steps or uses another detail, and look at the largest bottom gap in that layout before approving it.

Why do two vinyl estimates mention different post inserts?

They may use different systems with different structural requirements. Check each manufacturer's instructions and the gate-post detail rather than assuming an insert is always necessary or always unnecessary.

Is surface dirt a reason to paint a vinyl fence?

Start with the product's cleaning guidance. Dirt and surface chalk can often be washed away, while paint introduces a separate compatibility and warranty question. Test any approved cleaner on a small area first.
