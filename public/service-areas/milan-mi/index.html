Monroe and Washtenaw counties, Michigan · LOCAL FENCE PLANNING

Fence permits in the City of Milan

The city’s published April 2020 fence guide calls for a zoning permit for new, altered or relocated fences, with limited exceptions. Confirm current requirements with the city before work.

What the official guidance says

The guide lists side/rear limits of 6 feet for privacy and ornamental fencing and 5 feet for chain link and rail. It excludes fences from required front yards and gives corner lots a separate street-side rule. Applications include a plot plan or survey showing fences, structures, easements and setbacks, plus a dimensioned fence detail. Boundary-line work needs the city’s review of neighbor consent or surveyor documentation. The guide also addresses finished-side orientation, drainage and underground utilities. Repairs without structural changes and certain small additions have exceptions; confirm whether your project qualifies.

Prepare for the permit conversation
  • Property address and parcel number.
  • Available survey and a sketch of fence runs, gates, buildings and street edges.
  • Proposed material, height and approximate length.
  • Any association instructions, easements or pool-barrier requirements.

This is a suggested preparation list; the local office determines the required application documents.

Monroe County, Michigan · LOCAL FENCE PLANNING

Fence permits in Milan Township

Check the required permit and zoning review with Milan Township before installation. Section 13.08 requires site-plan approval outside Residential and Agricultural districts; do not interpret that distinction as a blanket residential permit exemption.

What the official guidance says

For residential dividing-line fences covered by section 13.08, the published limits are 4 feet in front yards and 6 feet in side or rear yards. Fences must also satisfy intersection-visibility rules. Agricultural zoning has separate location provisions, while other districts require site-plan review. The township currently links a zoning book updated in January 2022; ask the reviewing office about later amendments and any pool-enclosure requirements.

  • Available survey and a sketch of fence runs, gates, buildings and street edges.
  • Proposed material, height and approximate length.
  • Any association instructions, easements or pool-barrier requirements.

Monroe County, Michigan · LOCAL FENCE PLANNING

Fence permits in London Township

Section 6.102 requires zoning permit approval for construction, alteration or relocation of residential fences. Have the Zoning Administrator confirm the requirements and any parcel-specific exceptions before work begins.

What the official guidance says

The published ordinance generally limits front-yard fences to 4 feet and residential side/rear fences to 6 feet; those side/rear fences may not extend closer to the street than the front of the house. It also addresses corner sight lines, road rights-of-way, setbacks for taller view-obstructing fences and which way the finished side faces. Exceptions depend on land use, parcel characteristics and adjacent zoning, so do not assume a larger property is automatically exempt. Confirm current amendments with the township.
