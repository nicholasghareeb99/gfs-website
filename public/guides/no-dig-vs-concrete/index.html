No-Dig and Concrete Footings: Choosing for Your Fence

Compare installation approaches for your fence, gates and ground conditions. The right post system depends on the complete design and the property.

Installation methods

Choose the post system for the whole fence.

A driven post or anchor can reduce excavation where the system and site are suitable. Another footing may be needed for a particular fence design, gate, ground condition or access constraint. We review those details before recommending how your fence should be installed.

Design questions

Ask how the design addresses the site.

Review ground, materials, connections and access as parts of the complete installation.

01 / Ground & water

Ground and water: discuss drainage, ground movement and exposure along the proposed line.

02 / Materials & connections

Materials and connections: confirm the specified posts, rails, fasteners and finishes.

03 / Gate loads

Gate loads: an opening may need a different foundation or connection from the adjacent fence.

04 / Maintenance & access

Maintenance and access: ask about product care and how equipment will reach the line.

Written scope

Put the installation detail in writing.

  • Named system. Identify the proposed post or anchor and its requirements.
  • Compatible connections. Confirm how the fence attaches to its supports.
  • Site review. Discuss ground conditions, access and obstructions.
  • Gate detail. Confirm any different foundation or connection at an opening.
  • Access and cleanup. Put preparation and restoration responsibilities in the written scope.
Planning note

Fence appearance and foundation details are separate choices. Confirm the rail layout, visible style and post system in the same written quote.

Site-specific details

Where another detail may be needed.

An obstruction, unsuitable support, limited access or a demanding gate or fence design may change the recommendation. We will explain the proposed approach.

  • Ground conditions. Review support, drainage and the selected system requirements.
  • Fill and buried obstructions. Existing site conditions may change the installation detail.
  • Equipment access. Confirm how the proposed line can be reached and prepared.
  • Gate and fence loads. Review the openings, height and exposure before choosing foundations.
At your estimate

Ask which post or anchor is proposed, what requirements apply, and whether any gates or sections need another detail.

Bring these questions to your estimate.

Which system is proposed? How does it connect to the fence? What access and restoration work is included? Put the answers in the written scope.
